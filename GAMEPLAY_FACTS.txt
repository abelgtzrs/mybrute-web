keeps unchanged:

  packages/engine/vendor/glad.js    how brutes are built and level up

A few numbers were measured by running that code (for example "how many

  * "Level-up" means one level gained. A brute's displayed level is its

  * "Option 0 / option 1" are the two choices offered at each level-up.

===============================================================================
1. THE BRUTE'S STARTING POINT
===============================================================================

* Every brute starts with 2 strength, 2 agility, 2 speed and 2 life.
  Then it gets two bonus points of +1, each going to a stat picked at random.
  That makes 10 stat points in total, always.

* So the game has only 10 possible starting stat combinations. MEASURED over
  20,000 ids, exactly 10 appear. The five most common each cover
  about 12% of brutes, for example 3/2/3/2 or 2/2/3/3
  (strength/agility/speed/life).

* Starting health follows this formula (from the code):

      health = 50 + (life stat + level-ups x 0.25) x 6

  A new brute has 62 to 74 health. MEASURED: those are exactly the lowest and
  highest values in 20,000 ids.

* Health grows with level-ups even if you never add to the life stat: each
  level-up adds 0.25 to the life stat inside that formula, which is 1.5
  health.

* Every brute begins with bare hands ("HANDS") as its default weapon.


===============================================================================
2. THE RANDOM NUMBER GENERATOR BEHIND EVERYTHING
===============================================================================

* All the randomness in the game, for brutes and for fights, comes from one
  small formula that appears over and over in the code:

      seed = seed x 16807 mod 2147483647

  This is a well-known generator (often called "Park-Miller" or the
  "minimal standard" generator). 16807 is 7 to the 5th power, and 2147483647
  is 2 to the 31st power minus 1.

* Each brute has its own generator, started from the brute's id. That is why
  a brute's id fixes its whole career: its first level-up options, its
  hidden destiny (section 4), everything.

* Every fight has its own generator too, started from the fight seed. With
  the same seed and the same brutes, every hit lands (or misses) exactly the
  same way, every time.

* Choosing option 1 at a level-up does more than pick a reward. The code
  shifts the brute's generator seed by a number between 131 and 10,137. This changes
  every later offer. A brute's future is therefore a branching tree, not a
  line. Option 0 leaves the generator alone.

* Because of this, there are far more possible careers than there are brutes.
  A brute with 65 level-ups has 2 to the 65th power possible choice
  sequences, each leading to different offers after the first option 1.


===============================================================================
3. LEVEL-UPS: WHAT YOU ARE REALLY CHOOSING BETWEEN
===============================================================================

* At each level-up the game rolls one reward from the weighted table
  (section 5) and one stat. Then it builds your two options like this:

    - If the reward is new to this brute, the choices are:
        option 0 = the reward
        option 1 = +3 to a stat
    - If the roll gives a reward the brute already owns (or no reward), the
      choices are both stat boosts:
        option 0 = +3 to one stat
        option 1 = +2 to a second stat and +1 to a third

  So a reward is always option 0, and a stat boost is the other option.
  (The Lab's "avoid this reward" tool relies on this.)

* Early on, almost every level-up offers a reward. MEASURED: at the first
  level-up, a reward was offered for all 20,000 ids tested.

* Rewards get rarer as levels climb, for two reasons:
    - You can't be offered something you already own, and the table has only
      73 items, so duplicates become common.
    - From level 80 on, the game only rolls for a reward some of the time:
      it checks "random number mod level < 80". At level 160, that is a 50%
      chance. At level 400, it is a 20% chance.

* Stat boosts are bigger than they look. Raw stat points can be multiplied
  later by permanent bonuses (section 11), so early stat points are worth
  more to a brute that goes on to take a multiplier.

* "Super" stat bonuses (Super Force, Super Agility, Super Speed, Super Life)
  have a built-in limit. After the first one, the odds of the others drop
  from 60 to 3. After the second, to 1. After the third, to 0. So a brute can
  have at most 3 of the 4.


===============================================================================
4. EACH BRUTE HAS A HIDDEN "DESTINY"
===============================================================================

When a brute is created, the code uses its id to roll a private set of
restrictions. Nobody sees these directly, but they shape what the brute can
ever be offered.

* A 1 in 1000 chance of a special "stance" (the code stores a stance id of
  0 or 1). Presumably this changes how the brute stands in the animation;
  the engine only stores the number, so I can't say what it looks like.

* Four groups of rolls remove things from this brute's pool for good:

    Thief, Downpour, Hypno  Each is removed for 2 out of 3 brutes.
                            Only about 1 in 3 brutes can ever get each one.

    Immortality             Removed for 5 out of 6 brutes.

    The 8 oddball weapons   Leek, mug, frying pan, chick, trombone, keyboard,
                            noodles, racket: each is removed for 3 out of 4
                            brutes.

    0 to 2 ordinary         The game picks up to two weapons at random from
    weapons                 16 ordinary ones and removes them from this
                            brute's pool. (It picks with replacement, so it
                            may pick the same one twice and remove only one.)

* Two stats get an inborn lean. The brute gets a list of 11 "stat weights":
  two 0s, two 1s, two 2s, two 3s, and three random numbers from 0 to 3. The
  brute's two starting bonus points are drawn from this list. Stat numbers:
  0 strength, 1 agility, 2 speed, 3 life.

* Bear and panther are rivals. When a brute takes one of them, the other one
  is removed from its pool, except for a 1 in 1000 chance. MEASURED: out of
  5,000 ids taken to level 201 by always choosing option 0, none had both.

* Taking any talent removes all the other talents from the pool. A brute can
  only ever hold one.


===============================================================================
5. RARITY: HOW LIKELY EACH REWARD IS
===============================================================================

The base table has 73 rewards with weights adding to 1003 (before any
brute's destiny removes things). A reward's chance on one roll is its weight
divided by 1003:

  Group                Items   Total weight   Share of rolls
  Weapons                26         470           about 47%
  Permanent bonuses      27         411           about 41%
  Super skills           10          73           about 7%
  Pets                    5          32           about 3%
  Talents                 5          17           about 2%

The most common rewards:

  Sword             100   about 10.0%
  Knife              80   about  8.0%
  Stick              70   about  7.0%
  Super Force/Agility/Speed/Life   60 each   about 6.0% each
  Wood club          50   about  5.0%
  Lance              40   about  4.0%
  Axe                40   about  4.0%

The rarest rewards, with weight 1 (about 0.1% per roll):

  Immortality   Grab (super)   Bear   Panther
  Untouchable   Merciless      Diesel

Other rarities:
  * Hammer: weight 3 (0.3%).  Trapper: weight 4 (0.4%).
  * Dog 0: weight 20 (2.0%). Dog 1: weight 8. Dog 2: weight 2.

Notes:
  * The sword is about 100 times more common than the rarest rewards, and
    this is before destiny removes things, so some rare things are
    effectively even rarer.
  * MEASURED: when always choosing option 0 up to level 121 across 3,000
    ids, only about 2.6% of brutes ended up with Immortality.


===============================================================================
6. THE FOUR STATS AND WHAT THEY DO IN A FIGHT
===============================================================================

STRENGTH (damage)
  Brawl damage is:
      (weapon damage + strength x coefficient) x random 0.8 to 1.2
  where  coefficient = 0.2 + weapon damage x 0.05.
  Heavy weapons convert strength into damage much more efficiently. See
  section 7. After that the target's armor is subtracted, with a minimum of
  1 damage.

AGILITY (dodging and combos)
  - Dodge: the difference between your agility and the attacker's, times 2,
    adds to your dodge chance, limited to a swing of plus or minus 40
    percentage points.
  - Combos: each attack gives a chance to attack again equal to
        brute combo bonus + weapon combo + agility   (in percent)
    Every extra attack halves the chance for the next one.
  - Thrown weapons also add 0.15 x agility to their damage.

SPEED (how often you act)
  Each action pushes your next turn back by:
        weapon tempo x speed factor + a random 0 to 9
  and the speed factor is:   0.25 + 20 / (10 + speed) x 0.75
    speed 2     factor 1.50    (slow)
    speed 10    factor 1.00
    speed 50    factor 0.50
    speed 423   factor 0.28    (the factor can never go below 0.25)
  So speed has strongly diminishing returns, and a speed 423 brute acts
  about 5 times as often as a speed 2 brute with the same weapon.

LIFE (health)
  Health = 50 + (life stat + level-ups x 0.25) x 6. A brute with life stat
  379 at level 500 has 3,072 health (MEASURED, always choosing option 0, id 1).


===============================================================================
7. THE 28 WEAPONS
===============================================================================

The weapon table has 28 entries. 26 can be won as rewards. The other two are
HANDS (every brute's default) and FANGS (used by pets).

How to read the table: "damage" is the weapon's base damage, "speed" is its
tempo (lower is faster), and the rest are percentage adjustments.

The damage-per-hit column below uses the strength formula from section 6 with
the random factor at its average (1.0), at strength 10 and at strength 50.

  Weapon        Damage  Tempo   Hit @10 str  Hit @50 str
  Hammer          55     230        84.5        202.5
  Flail           36     220        56.0        136.0
  Big sword       28     180        42.0        110.0
  Axe             17     150        27.5         69.5
  Sword           10     120        17.0         45.0
  Knife            7      60        12.5         34.5

Things this table shows:

* A HAMMER at strength 10 hits for about 84, which is more than the 62 to
  74 health of a brand-new brute. One lucky hammer hit can end a level-1
  fight. The price: its tempo is 230 (very slow), it lowers your dodge by 40
  points, your parry by 50, and your combo chance by 40.

* The FLAIL has an enormous armor-piercing value (150) and is also very
  slow.

* Weapon "classes" matter for permanent bonuses. The code gives each weapon
  a class number (0 to 7):
      0 bare handed (hands, fangs, mug)
      1 blades (knife, sword, big sword, halberd, scimitar)
      2 piercing (lance, trident, stick, sai)
      3 axe
      4 blunt / heavy (hammer, clubs, flail, frying pan, trombone, keyboard,
        racket, leek)
      5 fan
      6 thrown (shuriken, chick, noodles)
      7 whip

* Each weapon has several percentage adjustments. Their code names mean:
    rip = riposte chance      rap = accuracy (lowers the target's dodge)
    dod = your dodge           par = your parry (block) chance
    per = armor piercing       dis = disarm chance (knock the enemy's weapon
    combo = combo chance            away)
    zone = reach, used in counters (section 13)

* The FAN is the speed weapon: tempo 28 (very fast), damage 4, dodge +60,
  riposte +50, combo +45, but parry -50 and disarm -50.

* The WHIP has the longest reach (zone 5), +30 accuracy, +30 dodge, +30
  disarm and +35 combo.

* The SAI has the best disarm: +100. Each hit has a 100% base chance to knock
  the opponent's weapon away.

* The LANCE has reach 3 and the HALBERD reach 4. Reach decides who wins
  "counter" duels (section 13).


===============================================================================
8. THE STRANGEST WEAPONS
===============================================================================

Eight weapons are far rarer, as a group, because each is removed from three
out of four brutes (section 4). They are also the odd ones out in the table:

  POIREAU (leek)    Riposte +100, accuracy +100, armor piercing +200,
                    combo +200. Damage only 5. The most extreme numbers of
                    any weapon in the table: it practically always strikes
                    back, never gets dodged, and chains attacks.

  RACKET            Riposte +100, parry +20. A weapon built to hit back.

  POELE (frying     Damage 17, parry +40 (the best block of any weapon),
  pan)              combo -40.

  MUG               Damage 8, accuracy +30, combo +40.

  TROMBONNE         Damage 20, tempo 250 (the slowest weapon), disarm +50,
  (trombone)        parry +20, reach 2.

  KEYBOARD          Damage 7, combo +50 (second only to the leek).

  POUSSIN (chick)   A thrown weapon: damage 5, tempo 32.
  NOODLES           A thrown weapon: damage 10, tempo 45.

The shuriken is the other thrown weapon (damage 3, tempo 12, the fastest
tempo in the game).

How throwing works:
* A "throw" weapon is thrown on every use. Its damage is
      (weapon damage + strength x 0.1 + agility x 0.15) x random 1.0 to 1.5
  with a minimum of 1. Thrown weapons are not lost after throwing.
* In this code a thrown weapon's damage is not reduced by the target's
  armor: the function that rolls it is called without the target. (Brawl
  hits, Downpour and grabs do subtract armor.)
* Any other weapon can also be thrown by accident. When a brute holds a
  melee weapon, there is a 1 in "damage" chance (for example 1 in 7 for a
  knife, 1 in 55 for a hammer) that it throws it instead. The weapon is then
  gone for the rest of the fight, and the brute is left with bare hands.
  Its damage uses the thrown formula above.


===============================================================================
9. THE TEN SUPER SKILLS
===============================================================================

How supers work:
* At the start of each of a brute's turns, the game draws one of its supers.
  The chance that one fires is roughly  (sum of the supers' "toss" values)
  / (that sum + 10), so the more supers a brute has, the more often it uses
  one.
* Each super has a limited number of uses ("uses" below). When the uses run
  out, the super is gone.
* Some supers refuse to fire when they would be useless, and then the brute
  carries on with a normal action.

  THIEF (2 uses, toss 8)
    Steals the opponent's current weapon, but only if they are holding one.
    If the thief already holds a weapon, it throws it away first, with a 1 in
    4 chance, otherwise the steal does not happen. The victim also loses a
    little time.

  BRUTE (1 use, toss 5)
    Doubles the damage of the brute's next action. The effect is removed at
    the end of that action.

  MEDECINE (1 use, toss 5)
    Heals between 25% and 50% of the health already lost, but only if at
    least 50 health is missing. Also cures poison on the team.

  NET (1 use, toss 5)
    Traps an opponent. A trapped target loses its dodge and parry. It can't
    act for  max(260 - 10 x sqrt(target strength), 50)  time units, and the
    first hit it takes breaks the net. Pets caught in a net are stuck for a
    very long time. The code prefers to net a pet if there is one.

  BOMB (2 uses, toss 2)
    Hits every enemy at once, pets included, for 15 to 24 damage each.

  GRAB (1 use, toss 2)
    A big grab: its damage is 4 times a "grab" hit, and a grab hit is
        (10 + strength x 0.6) x random 0.8 to 1.2.
    The brute must have bare hands to use it (or drop its weapon, a 1 in 4
    chance).

  SHOUT (2 uses, toss 8)
    Each enemy pet has a 50% chance to flee the fight. It only works if
    enemy pets exist.

  HYPNO (1 use, toss 3)
    Every enemy pet changes sides and fights for the hypnotist.

  DOWNPOUR (1 use, toss 2)
    Needs more than 2 weapons. The brute throws about half of its weapons
    (never the one in its hand) at the opponent in one go. Each weapon's damage is
    (weapon damage + strength x 0.1 + agility x 0.15) x random 1.0 to 1.5
    minus armor. It takes a long time (200 x speed factor).

  TRAPPER (4 uses, toss 20)
    Eats a dead pet to heal: 20% of maximum health for a dog, 30% for a
    panther, 50% for a bear. Only works if there is a body and the brute is
    missing at least 20 health. It has the highest toss value (20) and the
    most uses (4), so it fires often.

Some combinations:
* Hypno plus a team full of pets is strongest when the enemy has pets, since
  it flips all of them at once.
* Trapper works best with enemy or friendly pets dying, because it needs
  bodies.
* Shout and Hypno do nothing against a brute without pets.


===============================================================================
10. THE FIVE PETS
===============================================================================

Pets ("followers") join the fight as extra fighters. Each costs the owner
some of its life stat when taken, and fights with fangs (the bear uses bare
hands).

  Pet        Strength  Agility  Speed  Life  Starts acting at  Life cost
  Dog 0         6         5       3     -6        10             -2
  Dog 1         6         5       3     -6        10             -2
  Dog 2         6         5       3     -6        10             -2
  Panther      23        16      24     -4        60             -6
  Bear         40         2       1     10       360             -8

(Dog 0, 1 and 2 have identical numbers in the data table. The three dogs
look different but are tuned the same.)

Things to notice:
* The bear is extremely slow to start. Its starting time is 360, so it joins
  the action very late (a typical brute starts near 0). It has strength 40,
  the highest in the table.
* The panther is the fast one: speed 24, combo +60, dodge +20.
* A bear costs its owner 8 life stat points, which is a lot of health. A
  panther costs 6, a dog 2.
* The "Life" column is the pet's life stat. Its real health comes from the
  same formula as a brute's.
* Pets never get big stat bonuses; they don't level.
* A pet that dies leaves a body. A brute with Trapper can eat it
  (section 9), and the brute's team gets a "pet frag" counted in its stats.
* When a pet is hypnotised, it switches teams and the project's statistics
  code credits its later damage to the hypnotist's side.


===============================================================================
11. THE 27 PERMANENT BONUSES
===============================================================================

Names are the engine's own. Effects below come from the code.

STAT MULTIPLIERS
  SUPER_FORCE / SUPER_AGILITY / SUPER_SPEED / SUPER_LIFE
    +3 to that stat and then x1.5 to it at the end. At most 3 of the four
    can be taken (section 3). These have weight 60 each, the most common
    permanent bonuses.

  IMMORTALITY
    Life x3.5, but strength, agility and speed x0.75. Weight 1, and removed
    entirely for 5 of 6 brutes.

  DIESEL
    +5 speed and a x2.5 speed multiplier, but the brute starts acting 200
    time units late. A slow engine that wins the long fight. Weight 1.

OFFENCE
  BLADE_MASTER     +50% damage with blade-class weapons.
  BRAWL_MASTER     Doubles bare-hand damage (+100%).
  BLUNT_MASTER     Heavy weapons are used 25% faster.
  TWISTER          +20 combo chance.
  PUGNACITY        +30 riposte chance.
  CHOC             +50 disarm chance.
  MERCILESS        +30 accuracy, which lowers the target's dodge chance.
  VANDALISM        Each hit destroys one of the enemy's spare weapons.

DEFENCE
  SHIELD           +45 parry. The shield can be knocked off by a disarm.
  ARMOR            -5 damage from every hit and -10% speed (armor level +1).
  LEATHER_SKIN     -2 damage from every hit.
  UNTOUCHABLE      +30 dodge.
  VIGILANCE        +1 counter (counter chance is 10% per point, see
                   section 13).
  COUNTER          A parried hit lets the brute attack back at once, and
                   adds +10 parry.
  LEAD_BONES       Takes 30% less damage from blunt (heavy) weapons.
  IRON_HEAD        When an attack reaches this brute, there is a 30% chance
                   the attacker's weapon is knocked out of its hands.
  INCREVABLE       No single hit can take more than a fifth of maximum
                   health. (The name is a French word for "can't be
                   burst".)
  SURVIVAL         Once per fight, survives a fatal blow with 1 health.
  BALLERINA        The first incoming attack is always dodged.
  STAYER           70% chance to attack again right away after an attack is
                   dodged or parried.

TIMING
  WARM_BLOODED     Starts acting 200 time units earlier: first strike.

Combinations that stand out:
* Immortality plus a big life stat means huge health, and Diesel is the
  opposite plan: low early, strong late.
* Shield plus Counter plus a Frying Pan is a defensive build.
* A leek or racket (riposte 100) plus Pugnacity strikes back constantly.
* Armor takes 5 off each hit, and every hit still does at least 1 damage.
  So armor stops chip damage from weak weapons almost completely, and does
  little against a hammer.
* Vandalism is strong against Thief, Downpour and Hypno builds (they need
  spare weapons).


===============================================================================
12. HOW A FIGHT IS PLAYED, TURN BY TURN
===============================================================================

* A fight is a list of fighters sorted by an "init" number, a kind of clock.
  The fighter with the lowest init acts next. After acting, a fighter's
  init increases by its action's cost, so slow actions push it down the
  queue.

* The loop runs at most 1000 times. If neither side has won, the fight is a
  draw. In Classic rules a draw gives 1 XP, same as a loss.

* Each turn, in order:
    1. A support brute may join (section 14).
    2. The fighter may use a super skill.
    3. It may switch to a different weapon from its collection. When it
       switches, it keeps the new weapon for a while (a "keep" chance that
       halves each time the weapon is kept).
    4. It attacks: a brawl attack, or a throw.
    5. If its team is poisoned, it takes poison damage.
    6. Its init moves forward by the weapon's tempo x its speed factor.

* The target is picked at random from the enemy team. Pets can be targets.
  A netted enemy is avoided if there is any other target.

* Fights end when every non-pet, non-support brute on one team is dead.
  The project's fight player has a patched check for this so that 2v2 and
  larger teams work.

* MEASURED: 300 simulated fights between level-11 brutes, with ids drawn
  from two ranges and random level-up choices.
  The results were 165 wins for team 0 and 135 for team 1, with no draws.
  The shortest fight had 10 recorded events and the longest 141. The
  average was about 56. (This only tells you what a random pair of
  mid-level brutes looks like, not anything about balance.)


===============================================================================
13. HITS, DODGES, BLOCKS, COMBOS AND COUNTERS
===============================================================================

For every attack, the engine does these checks in this order:

  1. COUNTER (before the attack even happens)
     The defender gets a chance of
         (defender counter + defender reach - attacker reach) x 10%
     to hit first. So a long weapon (lance, halberd, whip) beats a short one
     at close range. This is why reach matters.

  2. DAMAGE is rolled (section 6).

  3. PARRY: the defender blocks with probability
         (defender parry + weapon parry - attacker weapon piercing) %.
     A block makes the damage 0.

  4. DODGE: the defender dodges with probability
         defender dodge + weapon dodge + agility swing
         - attacker accuracy - attacker weapon accuracy
     capped at 90%. A dodge overrides a block, and shows as damage -1 in the
     history.

  5. DISARM: if damage got through, the attacker may knock away the
     defender's weapon with probability
         (attacker disarm + weapon disarm) %.
     The defender is left with bare hands. If the defender has a shield, a
     separate roll on a wider scale (out of 300) may knock the shield off.

  6. HIT: the defender loses health.

  7. COMBO: the attacker may attack again (section 6, agility).

  8. RIPOSTE: afterwards, the defender may strike back with probability
         defender riposte + weapon riposte  (percent).

Other facts:
* A netted fighter can't parry or dodge.
* The history records every attack with its damage, and whether it was a
  disarm, a destroyed weapon or a broken shield. The project turns that log
  into per-brute statistics.


===============================================================================
14. TALENTS, THE SUPPORT BRUTE AND OTHER TEAM TRICKS
===============================================================================

The fight engine reads three extra settings per team ("team properties").
One of them is a whole extra fighter. As the next part explains, each of the
three is the in-fight half of a talent.

SUPPORT BRUTE (property name _st)   <- the Backup talent
  A team can hold a reserve brute that is not on the field at the start.
  Each time a fighter on that team acts, there is a chance equal to the
  fraction of health that fighter has lost that the reserve arrives.
    - The more hurt your fighter, the more likely help arrives.
    - The support brute stays for only 280 time units.
    - While present, damage to it doesn't reduce its health but its
      remaining time, at 5 time units per point of damage.
    - It leaves when its time is up, and a support brute dying doesn't count
      as a team member dying for the "who is alive" check.
  The project's Lab has fields to edit this brute.

POISON (property name _poi)   <- the Chef talent
  If a team is poisoned, each action of a fighter on that team costs it
  ceil(maximum health / 50) health, while the enemy is still alive.
  That is 2% of maximum health per action. Medicine on that team cures it.

SABOTAGE (property name _sab)   <- the Saboteur talent
  If set, one random weapon of every brute on the team is rigged. When the
  brute draws it, the weapon breaks on the spot: it is removed, the brute
  falls back to bare hands, and the turn is wasted (100 time units).

HOW THE TALENTS FIT TOGETHER
The talents work "out of combat": the website decides, before the fight
starts, whether a team gets one of the settings above. The fight engine only
carries out the result. The descriptions below come from the project owner,
who knows the original game. I matched each one to the engine's code.

  Talent (name in     Where it acts      What it does
  the game)
  Hyperactive         Outside fights     Raises the number of deaths a brute
                                         can suffer in a day before it can
                                         no longer train, from 3 to 4. Before
                                         the talent, a brute that died 3
                                         times had its training for the day
                                         over.
  Spy                 Outside fights     Lets you look into opponents' cells
                                         (their brute pages).
  Backup              In the fight       Lets the brute act as support for
                                         your other brutes that are higher
                                         level. This matches the support
                                         brute above.
  Chef                In the fight       Poisons the opponent: it loses 2% of
                                         its health per attack until its
                                         player drinks the tragic potion
                                         (Medecine). This matches the poison
                                         setting above.
  Saboteur            In the fight       Sabotages a weapon when the opponent
                                         takes it out. This matches the
                                         sabotage setting above.

  * The last three (Backup, Chef, Saboteur) each cost 1 death per day to
    activate. That ties them to the same death count Hyperactive extends,
    which is why Hyperactive is valuable to a brute that uses them.

What I checked in the code:
  * The 2% figure for Chef is exact: the poison damage is
    ceil(maximum health / 50), and 1/50 is 2%.
  * The engine has five talents: HYPERACTIVE, COOK, SPY, SABOTEUR and
    STRIKER. The owner's list has five too, with "Chef" for COOK and
    "Backup" in place of STRIKER. By elimination, I believe STRIKER is the
    engine's name for Backup, since support is the one fight setting left
    over. That is my inference; the code itself never links the two.
  * Hyperactive and Spy have no trace in the fight engine, which fits them
    working outside combat.
  * Talent weights in the reward table are 3 each (Hyperactive, Cook, Spy,
    Saboteur) and 5 for Striker.
  * Death counts and daily training limits are not part of this project's
    Classic mode. Classic uses 10 training fights per day and no death
    limit (README.md), so the talents' out-of-combat effects do not exist
    here. Only the fight half is supported, through the Lab's team
    settings.

===============================================================================
16. SMALL DETAILS WORTH KNOWING
===============================================================================

* Brute names in Classic opponents are just their ids. The id decides
  everything about the brute, so the name is a convenient label.

* The fight engine writes a history of events, and the animation plays that
  history. Event types in the code include: Attack, ThrowAttack, Steal,
  Grab, Bomb, Medecine, Net, Hypno, Escape, MoveTo, MoveBack, Death, Eat,
  Poison, Downpour, and "FxResistDamage" (shown when Increvable caps a hit).
  There are 23 event kinds in total.

* Stat numbers are truncated to whole numbers when multipliers are applied.
  A x1.5 on 11 strength gives 16, not 16.5.

* Dying to poison is possible: poison damage is applied by the same hit
  function as every other attack.

* A disarm removes the weapon from the victim's collection, not just from
  their hand. It is gone for the rest of the fight.

* A thief steals and then keeps the weapon for the rest of the fight.

* A hit on a netted fighter frees it, and then the fighter is delayed by 50
  time units before acting.

* Probabilities are rolled with the same generator in a fixed order. This
  is why fights replay identically, and why changing one early decision (an
  extra weapon, a different pet) can change the whole fight afterwards.

  happened on the original website. None of that is in the files this
  project has. Section 14 gives the details the owner supplied.

* The meaning of the "stance" id. The engine stores it, but I can't see
  what it changes.

* How the original website matched opponents, ranked players, or scheduled
  fights. Those parts are not in the recovered engine. This project's
  Classic mode uses its own rules for them (README.md).

* Balance. The numbers are real, but I haven't tried to rank builds. The
  measured fight results above are samples, not a verdict on what is
  strongest.
